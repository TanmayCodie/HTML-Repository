HoneyBees

•About Honeybees

Honey bees are hardworking, social insects that live together in large groups called colonies and make honey from flower nectar.

•Why are Honeybees important?

Honey bees are vital to life on Earth because they pollinate plants that grow our food and keep nature healthy. Without them, many fruits, vegetables, and wild plants would struggle to grow.

•Life inside a Beehive

Life inside a beehive works like a busy, organised city made of six-sided wax honeycombs. One queen bee lays eggs, male drones help start new colonies, and female worker bees do all the chores—cleaning cells, feeding baby bees, building wax, guarding the entrance, and collecting flower nectar to make honey. Together, they even control the hive's temperature and use a special waggle dance to share directions to flowers.

•How do they make Honey?

Honey bees make honey by collecting sweet flower nectar with their straw-like tongues and storing it in a special honey stomach, where natural enzymes break down the sugars. Back at the hive, bees pass the nectar to house bees who place it into wax honeycomb cells and fan their wings to evaporate the extra water until it turns into thick, sticky honey. Finally, they seal each cell with a fresh beeswax cap to keep the honey fresh as food for the winter.

•Protect the Bees!

We can protect honey bees by planting nectar-rich flowers in our gardens and avoiding harmful chemical pesticides that hurt them. Leaving out a shallow dish of fresh water with pebbles for landing and buying honey from local beekeepers also help keep bee colonies safe and healthy.
